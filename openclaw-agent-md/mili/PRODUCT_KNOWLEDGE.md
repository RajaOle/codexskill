## 9. Incoterms

Mili should always record the **named place/port**, not only the three-letter Incoterm.

Examples:

- `FOB Tanjung Priok, Incoterms® 2020`
- `CIF Rotterdam, Incoterms® 2020`
- `FCA supplier warehouse, Surabaya, Incoterms® 2020`

Never write simply “FOB” if the named port/place is known or required.

For every deal, track:

- Incoterm;
- named place/port;
- version/year if stated;
- freight responsibility;
- insurance responsibility;
- export clearance responsibility;
- import clearance responsibility;
- point of risk transfer.

Do not improvise legal interpretation when unclear; escalate.

## 12. Transaction Approval Gates

Before shipment/payment proceeds, the operating plan uses seven gates:

1. **Commercial viability** — real buyer, product/spec match, acceptable economics, delivery terms.
2. **Counterparty approval** — identity/ownership and required screening understood.
3. **Product & licence check** — entity can legally trade the commodity.
4. **Documentation** — purchase/sales contracts align on quantity, grade, Incoterm, payment terms.
5. **Financial approval** — working capital/trade-finance structure acceptable.
6. **Compliance approval** — no unexplained third-party payment, ownership, or document issue.
7. **Executive sign-off** — approved / approved with conditions / escalated / rejected.

Mili's job is to keep the gates visible and surface missing evidence.

## 13. Data Quality Rules

For live commercial records, Mili should always try to capture:

- date of quote/information;
- source/person;
- currency;
- unit;
- Incoterm/named place;
- validity;
- specification/grade;
- MOQ;
- quantity or capacity;
- lead time;
- payment terms;
- what remains unconfirmed.

A price without unit/date/Incoterm is incomplete commercial data.
